1. Aadhaar KYC API access — what you actually need

There are two routes, and only one is open to you.

Route A — direct UIDAI access (not for you)

Aadhaar eKYC that returns demographic data requires KUA or Sub-KUA status; authentication-only requires AUA or Sub-AUA. Realistically this tier is for banks, NBFCs, insurers and their authorised technology partners, and it carries licence fees, a security audit, IP whitelisting and an Aadhaar Data Vault obligation. A freelancer marketplace does not qualify on its own, and even the fast paths quote roughly 30 days. Do not spend a day of this week on it.

Route B — DigiLocker-based Aadhaar verification through an aggregator (do this)

The user consents in DigiLocker and you receive a digitally signed, tamper-evident e-Aadhaar — legally accepted for identity verification, no UIDAI licence needed on your side. Vendors to apply to today (apply to two in parallel; approval speed varies by reviewer): Sandbox (sandbox.co.in), Surepass, Digio, IDfy, Cashfree Verification Suite. Expect ₹3–7 per successful check and 3–10 working days for business verification.

Storage rules that apply the moment you accept an Aadhaar

Never store the full Aadhaar number in your app database. Persist only the masked last four digits plus a salted hash for de-duplication, and keep the signed XML/PDF in encrypted object storage with a 180-day retention rule. Log the consent artefact (timestamp, purpose text version, IP). Never render a full Aadhaar number in any UI, ticket, export or log line. Under the DPDP Act you also owe users a working deletion path — build the endpoint in week one, not after your first complaint.

4. Security checklist — all of it before 20 August

You are holding phone numbers, addresses, ID documents and payment mandates for people who work in strangers' homes. Treat each line as a launch blocker, not a nice-to-have.

Access & data

  • RLS enabled and policy-tested on every table — default deny, then allow
  • Service-role key server-side only; never in the client bundle or a public env var
  • ID documents in private buckets, short-lived signed URLs, no directory listing
  • Aadhaar/PAN encrypted at rest, masked in every UI and export
  • EXIF stripped from uploads; MIME and size validated server-side
  • Client phone numbers hidden until a freelancer accepts a lead

Abuse & money

  • OTP rate limit per phone and per IP (5/hour, 20/day) plus Cloudflare Turnstile
  • Lead-spam limits per client per day
  • Razorpay webhook signature verified; events processed idempotently by event id
  • Entitlement read from your DB, never from a client-supplied payment status
  • Admin routes behind RBAC + separate login; every action written to audit_log
  • No PII in logs, Sentry breadcrumbs or analytics events

Platform

  • HTTPS + HSTS, secure/httpOnly/sameSite cookies, CSP with no inline-script wildcard
  • Secrets in Vercel/Supabase env, rotated after launch; never committed
  • Daily point-in-time backups with one rehearsed restore
  • Dependency audit and lockfile pinned

Compliance

  • DPDP consent text versioned; purpose stated at collection
  • Working account-deletion and data-export path
  • Grievance officer name and email published (IT Rules)
  • Play Data Safety and Apple privacy labels match reality

5. Everything else on the list

Legal and commercial

  • Terms that make Sparx a listing platform, not a service provider or employer — no liability for work quality, no agency relationship
  • Subscription terms: monthly auto-renew, cancel anytime, no pro-rata refunds, clear GST treatment on the invoice
  • Freelancer agreement: accurate profile, own tools and taxes, no soliciting off-platform payments through you
  • Trademark search and filing for “FreelanceKar” (Class 35/42) — cheap now, expensive later
  • GST invoicing set up for the subscription revenue from day one

Native apps (after the web launch)

  • Wrap the PWA (Capacitor or Bubblewrap/TWA) rather than rebuilding — days, not weeks
  • Register the Play Console under Sparx Consultancy as an organisation, not a personal account: personal accounts must run a 12-tester, 14-day closed test before production, which alone would push you into September
  • Apple requires a D-U-N-S number for an organisation account — request it today; it is the long pole for iOS
  • Store assets: icon, feature graphic, 4–6 screenshots per platform, short and full description, support URL
  • Realistic dates: Play closed test 20 Aug, production ~1 Sep; App Store ~15 Sep

Go-to-market and operations

  • Launch one city, three or four categories, and hand-recruit the first 50 freelancers — a marketplace with empty categories converts nobody
  • Free first month for founding freelancers, priced plan visible from day one so the paywall is never a surprise
  • Support inbox with a stated response time, plus a WhatsApp number you actually answer
  • Instrument four numbers only: signups, listings live, paid subscriptions, leads delivered
  • Google Business Profile, city landing pages for SEO, and a simple referral ask in the freelancer dashboard
